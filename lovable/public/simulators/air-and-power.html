    Why the air matters most

    Carbon dioxide

    Each person breathes out about 18 litres of CO₂ an hour. In a closed room it builds up long before oxygen runs short. Above 1% people feel drowsy, at 3% they get headaches and pant, and above 5% it is dangerous.

    Heat

    Every person gives off about as much heat as an 80 W bulb, and every appliance ends up as heat too. Deep rock stays at 11 to 13 °C all year, but it soaks up heat slowly, so a crowded shelter warms up over days.

    The Fortes answer

    Blast valves and NBC filters bring in clean outside air and keep the shelter at slight overpressure. A battery bank runs the fans, with two generators and stored fuel to recharge it, and a hand-crank fan if everything else fails.

    Educational model only. Each person produces 0.018 m³ of CO₂ and uses 0.021 m³ of O₂ an hour at rest, and gives off 80 W of heat. Air volume is 80% of floor area × 2.6 m. Heat flows to rock at 11 to 13 °C through the walls and out with the ventilation air; electric heating and cooling run through a heat pump while there is power. The generator runs when the battery falls below 25% and stops at 95%, using 0.3 litres of diesel per kWh. With no power the fans stop, and people turn a hand-crank fan for about 3 m³/h each. Limits: UK workplace exposure limit for CO₂ 5,000 ppm (8 hours); oxygen deficiency below 19.5%. Real shelters vary with design, airtightness, humidity and activity.
