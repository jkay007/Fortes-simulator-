The 7–10 rule

For every sevenfold increase in time after the blast, fallout radiation drops tenfold. The first 48 hours are the most dangerous, which is why you need to be sealed in before the fallout arrives, not after.

Educational model only. Blast, heat and initial radiation use cube-root scaling from published weapons-effects data (Glasstone & Dolan). Fallout uses a simplified elliptical plume and the t-1.2 decay law, with a steady wind and flat terrain. Real outcomes depend on height of burst, weather, terrain, soil and the exact shelter specification. Shelter depths, survivable yields and protection figures are illustrative, not a Fortes guarantee. Direct-hit survival uses Glasstone & Dolan crater scaling and treats twice the crater depth as the damage zone.
