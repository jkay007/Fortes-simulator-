How close does the shelter have to be?

Minutes or days

There are two kinds of warning. Minutes is a missile already in the air: only a shelter you can reach in that time counts, so it has to be close to where you live or work. Days is tension building in the news: distance hardly matters, because you travel early and stay. That is the case for evacuation transport, and for treating the move as planned rather than panicked.

If you will not make it

Do not set off on a journey you cannot finish. Get inside the nearest solid building, go to the basement or the middle of it, away from windows, and stay there. Fallout takes time to arrive, so the first minutes indoors matter most. US guidance: get inside, stay inside, stay tuned.

Educational model only. Flight times are commonly quoted round figures: about 30 minutes for an intercontinental ballistic missile, and 10 to 15 minutes for a missile launched from a submarine close to the coast. The time an alert takes to reach the public is not published and will vary by country and by system, so it is yours to set. Travel uses steady average speeds and assumes the route is open; real traffic after an alert is unknown. The model ignores where the missile lands relative to you and the shelter. Choosing a site away from likely targets matters as much as reaching it: see the Location Risk Checker. Not a FORTES guarantee. In an emergency, follow your local authorities.
