# Blast Ratings Explained: What 15 to 100 PSI Stands Up To

*Every FORTES tier carries a published blast rating. A new free tool shows what those numbers mean on the ground: how close a weapon can land before each one is passed.*

Every FORTES tier has a published blast rating: 15 PSI for Starter, 25 for Origin, 50 for Core, 75 for Titan and up to 100 for Elite. A number like that is easy to print and hard to picture. So we have built a free tool, [Blast Ratings Explained](/simulators/blast-ratings), that turns each rating into a distance.

## What PSI measures

A nuclear explosion sends a blast wave out through the air. As it passes, the air pressure jumps above normal for a moment. That jump, measured in pounds per square inch, is the overpressure, and it falls away quickly with distance.

The same scale describes ordinary buildings. At about 1 psi, windows shatter. At about 5 psi, most houses collapse. At about 20 psi, concrete buildings are destroyed. Against that, Starter's 15 PSI is three times what brings down a house, and Elite's 100 PSI is five times what destroys a concrete building.

## What a rating covers

A blast rating is the peak overpressure a structure is designed to take. The blast wave travels through the air, so for a shelter deep underground, what meets it is everything at the surface: the entrance, the blast doors and the air intakes. The rooms below are protected by the earth above them. That is a separate question of depth and cratering, and the [Depth Explorer](/simulators/depth-explorer) covers it. FORTES ratings are published per tier and are subject to final site engineering.

## How close it can land

The tool draws the blast pressure against distance from ground zero and marks each tier's rating on it. Where the curve crosses a rating is the closest a weapon can land before that rating is passed.

For a 300 kt ground burst, the size of a typical ICBM warhead and the hard case for any rating, it looks like this:

| Tier | Rating | Closest it can land |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FORTES Starter | 15 PSI | about 1.7 km |
| FORTES Origin | 25 PSI | about 1.4 km |
| FORTES Core | 50 PSI | about 1.1 km |
| FORTES Titan | 75 PSI | about 930 m |
| FORTES Elite | Up to 100 PSI | about 850 m |
| A typical house | 5 psi | about 3.2 km |

The last row is the point. The same weapon collapses most houses out to about 3.2 km. The area in which houses come down is about 14 times the area inside which the Elite rating is passed.

Smaller and larger weapons scale the same way. A 15 kt ground burst, the size of the Hiroshima bomb, has to land within about 310 m to pass 100 PSI and within about 630 m to pass 15 PSI. A 1 Mt ground burst has to land within about 1.3 km to pass 100 PSI, while houses collapse out to about 4.8 km.

## Ground burst or airburst

The tool lets you switch between a weapon that bursts on the ground and one that bursts high in the air. An airburst spreads moderate pressure over a wider area, so it brings houses down further out: about 4.7 km for 300 kt. But the peak on the ground is lower. In the model, a 300 kt airburst passes the Starter rating within about 2.3 km and the Core rating within about 820 m, and does not reach 100 PSI on the ground at all. A lower burst would push that peak higher, which is why the ground burst is the case to plan for.
